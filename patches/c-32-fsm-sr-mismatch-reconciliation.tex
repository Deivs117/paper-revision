% --- prose after the table, ~line 523 ---
\revblue{In the three single-stimulus suites (Appetitive, Aversive, Obstacle), the FSM's fixed priority rule is structurally well-matched to the task --- one stimulus is active at a time, so a static hierarchy resolves it immediately and deterministically. Both controllers eventually complete every mission in all three suites (100\% mission-level success in Table~\ref{tab:fsm_comparison}, i.e.\ a trial that first times out and is then retried still counts as a success once it lands). The two controllers diverge, however, in how often that retry is needed: the FSM never requires one, at any noise level, while the Neural system occasionally does as noise increases. Figure~\ref{fig:sr_vs_noise_level} makes this per-attempt view explicit --- Neural's success rate \emph{per individual attempt} (not deduplicated by retry) dips to 60\% in Appetitive and 75\% in Aversive at the highest noise levels, even though every one of those missions is ultimately completed on a later attempt.}

% --- prose, ~line 543 ---
\revblue{The Neural-side values recomputed here for the Appetitive, Aversive, Obstacle, and Complex suites are independently derived from raw trial logs and, as of this revision, match Table~\ref{tab:consolidated_simulation_metrics}'s figures for the same four suites on every reported statistic, including success rate: both tables count a suite as a success once the mission lands, regardless of any earlier timed-out attempt on the same trial. This is a deliberately different accounting from the per-attempt success rate discussed above and shown in Figure~\ref{fig:sr_vs_noise_level}, which does not deduplicate retries --- the two views answer different questions (did the mission ultimately succeed, vs.\ how often does an individual attempt fail as noise increases) and are not in conflict.}
