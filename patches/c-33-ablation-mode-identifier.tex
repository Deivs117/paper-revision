\revblue{To empirically ground this architectural choice, rather than relying solely on the citations above, an ablation study was conducted on the basal-ganglia circuit itself, introducing four ablation variants of the simulated circuit: the intact circuit (\emph{full}, control); \emph{no lateral inhibition}, which zeroes the cross-channel inhibitory terms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:stn}--\eqref{eq:str} (Gpe$\rightarrow$STN, STN$\rightarrow$Gpi) while leaving same-channel dynamics untouched; \emph{no STN/STR}, which removes the STN and STR layers entirely, routing the raw stimulus directly into GPe/GPi with the same per-channel gains STN would otherwise apply; and \emph{threshold-only}, which bypasses the STN/GPi/GPe/STR dynamics altogether in favor of a static fixed-priority threshold over the raw stimulus --- the same threshold-logic family already used elsewhere in this architecture's own Locomotion Module. Each variant was evaluated across the Appetitive Targeting (no-conflict control) and Complex Navigation (three simultaneous, conflicting stimuli) scenarios ($n=12$ replicates/variant/scenario); results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.}
