In scenarios presenting simultaneous appetitive and aversive stimuli, the basal-ganglia-inspired winner-take-all arbitration module (Section~\ref{sssec:Basal_Ganglia_Module}) \revblue{consistently prioritized the most relevant objective, its predominant channel activation inhibiting competing channels and reducing the probability of erratic trajectory changes; this behavior yielded direction-switching neural latencies of 184--534~ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:FourPanelLatencies}).} The alignment between decision-neuron activity peaks and executed maneuvers supports the hypothesis that a basal-ganglia-inspired architecture combined with multimodal sensory processing offers clear advantages in environments where goal prioritization is critical for mission success.
