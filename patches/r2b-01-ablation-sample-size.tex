\revblue{These conclusions should be read in light of the study's sample size. Each combination of variant and scenario comprises $n=12$ simulated trials, a budget sufficient to detect the large effects reported above but one that leaves limited statistical power for smaller ones. Against the intact circuit's $12/12$ successes, Fisher's exact test at this sample size reaches $p<0.05$ only when an ablated variant fails in at least five of its twelve trials; the \emph{threshold only} variant's $83.3\%$ success rate ($10/12$, exact 95\%~CI $[51.6, 97.9]\%$) is therefore statistically inconclusive rather than evidence of equivalence with the intact circuit, and the effects lying closest to the significance boundary (its decision-latency increase, $p=0.014$, and Roll RMS increase, $p=0.017$) should be regarded as provisional until replicated. The two mechanism-removing ablations, by contrast, fail in eight (\emph{no STN/STR}) and nine (\emph{no lateral inhibition}) of twelve trials, well beyond that boundary, so their collapse is not an artifact of the limited sample. Larger-scale repeated trials are left as future work to tighten these estimates and confirm the smaller effects. These would include more replicates per condition, a wider range of conflict configurations and noise levels, and an equivalent ablation protocol on the physical platform, which the present study does not include.}
